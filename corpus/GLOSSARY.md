# Pharmacology Glossary

Short definitions for terms that appear in the corpus. Written for a reader who codes but has not
studied pharmacology. This file ships with Attachment B.

## Core concepts
- **Indication**: an official reason a drug is approved for use (e.g., "indicated for hypertension").
- **Contraindication**: a condition that makes using a drug unsafe (e.g., "contraindicated in pregnancy").
- **Dosage**: the prescribed amount, frequency and route (e.g., "500 mg PO every 6 hours").
- **Route**: how the drug enters the body: PO (oral), IV (intravenous), IM (intramuscular), SC
  (subcutaneous), topical, inhaled.
- **PRN**: Latin "pro re nata", meaning "as needed" rather than scheduled.
- **OTC**: over-the-counter — available without prescription.

## Pharmacokinetics (what the body does to the drug)
- **Half-life (t½)**: the time for the drug's plasma concentration to fall by half.
- **CYP enzymes (e.g. CYP3A4, CYP2D6)**: liver enzymes that metabolize most drugs. Inhibitors raise
  drug levels, inducers lower them. A frequent source of interactions.
- **First-pass metabolism**: for oral drugs, how much is destroyed by the liver before reaching
  circulation.

## Pharmacodynamics (what the drug does to the body)
- **Mechanism of action (MoA)**: the molecular target and effect (e.g., "COX-1/2 inhibitor").
- **Agonist / antagonist**: activates / blocks a receptor.
- **Therapeutic window**: the gap between effective and toxic doses. Narrow for warfarin, wide for
  acetaminophen below hepatotoxic levels.

## Safety and monitoring
- **Boxed warning (previously "black box")**: the strongest FDA warning on a label.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: liver damage. Risk for acetaminophen above 4 g/day in adults.
- **Nephrotoxicity**: kidney damage. Risk for NSAIDs, aminoglycosides.
- **Serotonin syndrome**: potentially fatal reaction from combining serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, MAOIs,
  tramadol, triptans).
- **Reye's syndrome**: rare, severe condition linked to aspirin in children with viral infection.
- **INR (International Normalized Ratio)**: a blood test monitoring warfarin's effect.
- **NSAID**: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug. Ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin.
- **DOAC**: direct oral anticoagulant. Apixaban, rivaroxaban. Alternative to warfarin.
- **SSRI**: selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor. Sertraline, fluoxetine.
- **MAOI**: monoamine oxidase inhibitor. Older antidepressant class with many interactions.

## Reading drug labels
- **"Highlights of Prescribing Information"**: the one-page summary at the top of a DailyMed label.
- **Section numbers (DailyMed)**: 1 Indications, 2 Dosage, 4 Contraindications, 5 Warnings and
  Precautions, 7 Drug Interactions, 12 Clinical Pharmacology.
- **Patient information leaflet**: the lay-language version, usually at the end of the label.
